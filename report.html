MoveInSync · Forward Deployed Engineer case study · employee app (Play Store)

Why the Play Store rating is 4.64, and what to fix first

The app is already well rated, so the lost points sit in a small group of unhappy users: 6.4% of ratings are 1★. Their written reviews point to reliability and access (38%), tracking and cab operations (25%) and booking (12%). Fix the first two, add a release safety net, and start answering reviews.

What to do first

Do these three first, in this order.
  1. Make the app stay logged in and stop failing. Reliability and access problems (crashes, slowness, login or OTP failures, forced logouts) appear in 38% of 1-2★ reviews. This is the biggest and most fixable block, and it is all Engineering.
  2. Fix live tracking and tell people what is happening to their cab. Tracking, late cabs and routing appear in 25% of 1-2★ reviews. Part of this is operations, but the app can still show a reason and a new ETA.
  3. Put a safety net around releases and answer the reviews. Staged rollouts are normally a small engineering change, and weekly review replies need no engineering at all; only 4.2% of 1-2★ reviews get a reply today.
Be realistic about the number. The app is already at 4.64 from 114,879 ratings, because 85.5% of ratings are 5★. The gap is the 6.4% of ratings that are 1★ (7,317 people). Reaching 4.8 would need about 4,629 of them (63%) to become 5★. Even if every 1-2★ rating became 4★ the score would be 4.84. Fixing the main areas (reliability, tracking and cab ops, booking, support) for half of the affected people is worth roughly +0.07 on the lifetime score, if they then rate 4★. Because the lifetime number moves slowly, track the share of written reviews that are 1-2★ as the leading indicator. It is 28.6% overall and was already about 22% in the app's four best months, so 22-23% is a credible target.

Prioritised recommendations

Ranked by modelled impact against effort. "Lift" is the modelled change in the lifetime rating if half of the affected users moved to 4★, up to all of them moving to 5★. Themes overlap, so lifts are not additive.

P1 · do first Keep people logged in; stop crashes, hangs and blank screens

Problem
Users are logged out repeatedly, cannot get a login OTP, or hit slow, frozen or blank screens that only a reinstall fixes.
Evidence
38% of 1-2★ reviews (3,068 ratings if representative). Forced logout/re-login: 134 reviews. Slow/lag/hang/crash words: 164. Login OTP or code not received: 92. Reviewers say they had to reinstall or clear data: 59. Crash-type complaints rose from 14.7% to 22.0% of negatives in the last 90 days (borderline signal, z≈1.9).
Fix
(a) Keep sessions alive with refresh tokens and no forced re-auth within a set period (e.g. 30 days) unless security requires it. (b) Add a second OTP channel (email or WhatsApp), a resend timer and delivery-failure monitoring on the SMS route. (c) On app start, detect corrupted local state and self-reset instead of asking users to reinstall. (d) Review the top 10 crash and ANR clusters in Play Console / Crashlytics and fix them in order of affected users.
Owner
Engineering (mobile + auth backend)
Success
Reliability-and-access share of 1-2★ reviews from 38% to under 25% in 8 weeks; "logged out" and "OTP not received" reviews halved; crash-free sessions above 99.5%.
Modelled lift
+0.04 to +0.10 on the rating

P2 · do first Make live tracking reliable and explain delays in the app

Problem
Location updates lag or jump, the map keeps resetting, and when a cab is late or changes, the user is not told why. Users blame the app even when the cause is operational.
Evidence
25% of 1-2★ reviews mention tracking, late or no-show cabs, or routing. Tracking words appear in 89 reviews, calling or chatting with the driver in 49, no-show disputes in 42, map auto-zoom resets in 12. One of the most up-voted reviews says calling the driver is blocked by a "company policy" message, and the reviewer missed a bus.
Fix
(a) Raise the location refresh rate during the last 15 minutes before pickup, and show "last updated Xs ago". (b) Stop the map from resetting the zoom every 30 seconds; focus on the cab until pickup, then the destination. (c) When a cab is delayed or reassigned, show the reason and a new ETA. (d) Fix in-app call/chat with the driver, including the policy-blocked case. (e) Show proof of waiting and a one-tap dispute for no-shows.
Owner
Engineering + Product; Ops for the cab-delay reasons
Success
Tracking and late-cab share of 1-2★ reviews from 25% to under 18%; no-show dispute reviews halved; call-failure tickets down.
Modelled lift
+0.03 to +0.07 on the rating

P3 · do first Release safety net

Problem
Negative reviews cluster around specific releases, and changes arrive without notice.
Evidence
In 2026-10, 8 of 25 negative reviews (32%) mention an update, against 7% over the whole period (an early signal on a small number, coinciding with the latest build, v12.19.0). Two 2026 dips (June and August) reached 32-34% negative, against about 22% in March-May and July. They fall on builds 12.13-12.14 and 12.16-12.17, but release dates are needed to confirm a link.
Fix
(a) Staged rollout: 5% → 20% → 50% → 100% with automatic halt if the crash rate or the 1-2★ share rises. (b) Show a short "what's new" note for behaviour changes (for example the seat-belt prompt before the boarding OTP, or the tracking-window change). (c) Put risky changes behind remote flags so they can be switched off. (d) Share the release calendar with the ratings dashboard so each dip can be tied to a build.
Owner
Engineering + Product
Success
No month above 30% 1-2★; update-mentioning share of negatives under 8% in the month after each release.

P4 Booking and cancellation integrity

Problem
Schedules do not show, bookings are cancelled or moved to a waitlist without notice, and users are marked no-show when they were not at fault.
Evidence
12% of 1-2★ reviews (191 reviews).
Fix
Send a push notification and an in-app banner for every automatic status change (cancellation, waitlist, reassignment), with the reason. Add an appeal path for no-show marks. Fix desk/seat selection bugs.
Owner
Product + Engineering; Ops for no-show policy
Success
Booking share of 1-2★ reviews from 12% to under 8%.
Modelled lift
+0.01 to +0.03 on the rating

P5 · quick win Answer the reviews that matter

Problem
Almost no replies, and the ones that exist are generic thanks to happy users.
Evidence
152 replies across 5,712 reviews (2.7%); only 4.2% of 1-2★ reviews got one. Helpdesk unreachable or no response is raised in 61 negative reviews.
Fix
Reply to every 1-2★ review within 48 hours using theme-specific templates (login help, tracking, booking) that give the next step and an in-app support route. Do not ask users to change their rating. Review the replies weekly.
Owner
Support / Product (no engineering needed)
Success
At least 60% of 1-2★ reviews replied within 48 hours; support-themed share of negatives falls from 6%.
Modelled lift
+0.01 to +0.02 on the rating, plus some users edit their review after a reply

P6 Rating prompt policy

Problem
Some users are asked to rate or give feedback after every ride, with no way to skip.
Evidence
24 negative reviews complain about it (small but irritating). Written reviews run far more negative than the rating base (29% vs 7.1%), so happy riders are under-represented.
Fix
Ask for a Play Store rating only after a successful trip, not on every ride and at most once per 90 days, with a clear "Not now". Keep a separate in-app trip feedback form and a "Report a problem" button on the trip-end screen. Do not hide the store review option from unhappy users, because steering unhappy users away from the store can conflict with Google Play's review guidelines.
Owner
Product
Success
Rating-prompt complaints near zero; more 5★ ratings from happy riders. As an illustration, 10% more 5★ ratings would add about +0.03 (assumption, not forecast).

P7 Small fixes that users keep raising

Backlog
  • Terms and agreement pop-up shown again on every open (12 reviews).
  • QR scan problems: trip not marked as started, blurry camera, torch left on (23 reviews).
  • Clearer "Login" and "Logout" labelling for shift times, which night-shift users find confusing.
  • Dark mode, a clearer feedback form, and chat or call with the driver (requests).
Owner
Product + Engineering

How this is tracked every week

The repository includes a scheduled GitHub Action. Every Monday it re-scrapes the Play Store, re-tags the reviews, and rebuilds the dashboard page: current rating and histogram, 1-2★ share by month, theme shares, which themes are rising in the last 90 days, and a version table. The question "How are our ratings?" is answered by one link.

How I got here, and the limits

  • Scope. Employee app com.moveinsync.ets (4.64, 114,879 ratings). Driver Partner (3.5) has only 46 ratings and 7 written reviews, too few to analyse; it is a footnote.
  • Data. Public Play Store data via google-play-scraper: the app snapshot (rating, exact histogram, version) and 5,712 written reviews from 22 Oct 2024 to 11 Oct 2026. Written reviews are about 4.0x more negative than the full rating base, so they show what unhappy users say, not what the average user feels.
  • Tagging. A rule-based tagger (keyword rules, free, repeatable) assigns up to two themes per review from a 16-tag taxonomy built around the employee journey. I hand-labelled 100 reviews (36 labels were revised after a second-opinion pass with Claude), then split them 50 for tuning and 50 held out. On the held-out 50: main theme exactly right 64%, right within the top two themes 78%, agreement (kappa) 0.60. On the tuning set it reached 84%, which is optimistic. Spot checks: forced-logout tag 14 of 18 correct (78%), cab-ops tag about 11 of 18 correct (~60%). Treat theme shares as roughly ±10 percentage points and rely on the grouped numbers.
  • 29% of negative reviews say nothing usable (for example "worst app"), and are shown as "No usable detail".
  • Modelled lift assumes the themes in written 1-2★ reviews are representative of all 1-2★ ratings. That is an assumption, not a measurement.
  • Tools. Python (pandas, google-play-scraper, scikit-learn), regular-expression tagging, GitHub Actions for the weekly run, and Claude as an assistant for planning, code and drafting. An LLM tagger notebook is also included and can replace the rules.
  • With a week of access to the ops team I would match each theme to ticket data, get the release calendar and crash dashboards, and interview five riders and two transport desk staff to see which complaints are app problems and which are operations.
Numbers are computed from public Play Store data; see the repository README for how to rerun them.
